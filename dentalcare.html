Communication Barriers in Dental Care

In the globalization of the world, more and more non-native English speakers are coming to the UK and many of these patients face communication challenges in accessing healthcare.

The project aims to improve the dental experience for non-native English-speaking patients by providing functions such as disease self-examination and oral modelling in the app to enhance patients' confidence in the process.

Year:
2023

Duration:
7 months

Platform:
Mobile

Role:
UX designer

Tools & Skills:
Figma; User Research; Prototype; User Test

Problem Statement

Improve the user experience of Non-native English-speaking patients, making patients feel more confident in their communication.

Research

Interview & Persona

● Aim: Understanding the communication challenges faced by non-native English-speaking patients in receiving dental treatment.
● User Research: Conducted in-depth user interviews with 10 non-native English speakers to understand their pain points in dental communication. Created detailed personas and empathy maps that highlighted specific challenges faced by these users.

User Journey:

Based on the interview, I designed the following user journey, during different stages, users encounter different challenges.
● User Journey Mapping: Mapped out the user journeys to identify critical touchpoints where communication breakdowns occur, analyzing patient interactions with dental staff before, during, and after appointments.

Current Solutions:

Insights from research:

● Difficulty in accurately describing their illness
● Difficult to understand some of the medical knowledge

Design

Information Architecture:

● Information Architecture: Designed and tested the app's information architecture using tree testing with 30 participants to ensure users can easily find the information they need. Based on the test results, feedback was used to refine the structure, leading to more intuitive navigation and better information flow.
● Tree Testing: After optimizing the information architecture, the average success rate for five tasks increased from 44% to 81%.

Lo-Fi & Task-based User Testing:

Based on the improved information architecture, we designed low-fidelity (Lo-Fi) to further test the enhancements and validate whether the app effectively conveys the patient's illness, thereby boosting their confidence in medical treatment. A task-based user testing approach was employed to assess the effectiveness of the Lo-Fi design. The following picture illustrates the improved Lo-Fi.
● Prototyping: Developed low-fidelity wireframes to visualize initial ideas. Conducted task-based usability testing with 10 users on key tasks, such as scheduling appointments and understanding medical instructions.
● Task-Based Usability Testing: based on prototyping, over 80% of participants successfully completed critical tasks, leading to further iterations based on their feedback.

Hi-Fi & SUS Testing:

The high fidelity is designed based on the improved low fidelity and SUS testing is taken to test the usability of the app and finally the improved high fidelity based on the user feedback is as follows.
● SUS Testing: The app achieved a System Usability Scale score of 82/100, indicating above-average user satisfaction and usability.

Conclusion

● The current app can help patients to a certain extent by increasing their confidence in helping them express their need for access to healthcare.
● However, the app still remains insufficient during actual clinical encounters. For first-time patients, tangible diagnostic procedures such as X-rays remain essential, and the disease self-examination system can only be used as an assist.
● In addition, the interface will be refined as the research progresses and efforts will be made to conduct rigorous validity evaluations, such as clinical impact assessments, to determine its clinical applicability.
